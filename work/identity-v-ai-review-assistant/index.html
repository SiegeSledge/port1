Overview

During a summer UX design internship at NetEase Interactive Entertainment, I worked on two things for Identity V: an AI post-match review assistant for new players, designed from scratch, and a backpack quick-swap interaction for Mojin mode.

Project 1: AI post-match review assistant

The problem

New players can perform the basic actions but struggle to understand the decisions behind a match. Raw replays and text summaries do not tell them where they went wrong or what to practice.

The design

  • Key match events, game-mechanic knowledge and personalized advice are organized into a closed loop: locate the problem, explain why, suggest training.
  • I delivered the core flow, an interaction prototype, a working demo and a multi-agent architecture.
  • In the demo, the AI reads structured match events through Function Calling on a preset event viewer, so its analysis is tied to specific behavior.
  • For talent recommendation and explanation, RAG retrieves the wiki, game terminology and skill counter / combo information, so suggestions combine real match evidence with domain knowledge.

Natural language versus GUI

I explored how language and graphical interface should divide the work in an AI-coach scenario. Natural language handles explanation, follow-up questions and personalized advice. Timestamps, key events and structured information help the player locate the problem, which avoids squeezing a complex match into a text answer that cannot be verified.

Project 2: Backpack quick swap in Mojin mode

The problem

In high-pressure combat the player needs to decide quickly but cannot afford a mis-tap.

The design

  • Item recommendation, preview and confirm, one-tap swap and error-protection mechanisms.
  • Recommendation rules, system states and data requirements defined for engineering.
  • The design moved into test-server validation.

How I worked

  • Held prototype reviews and version validation with design, engineering and art, and followed experience issues through to closure.
  • Wrote interaction flows, recommendation logic and data-interface requirements.
  • Combined AI agents, Figma and code-generation tools for requirements analysis, prototyping and validation, which sped up iteration and cross-team collaboration.
  • Independently wrote the patent-disclosure materials for the UI interaction.
